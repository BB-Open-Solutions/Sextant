# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be straightforward and be kind. Those are not in tension.

## What that means here

Sextant is a small project. Most conversation is about whether something works,
why it broke, and what to do instead. That conversation goes well when people
can say "this is wrong, and here is what I measured" without it being an
attack, and hear it without taking it as one.

So:

**Criticise the work, not the person.** "This drops the error" is useful. "You
never check errors" is not, and it makes the next person quieter.

**Say what you actually think.** Softening a real problem until it disappears
helps nobody. A clear "I think this is the wrong approach, because…" is more
respectful than agreement you do not mean.

**Assume the other person had a reason.** Code that looks strange is usually
either a constraint you cannot see or an old fix nobody wrote down. Ask before
concluding.

**Be honest about certainty.** "I measured this" and "I think this" are
different claims, and mixing them up costs somebody an afternoon. This applies
to maintainers most of all.

**Nobody owes you their time.** Not a review, not a merge, not an answer today.
Persistence is fine; entitlement is not.

## Not welcome

Harassment, personal attacks, and demeaning comments about who somebody is
rather than what they wrote — including about nationality, gender, sexuality,
disability, age, religion or background. Deliberately making someone
uncomfortable and calling it directness. Sustained disruption after being asked
to stop.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, chat and at events
where you are representing the project.

## If something goes wrong

Mail **conduct@bb-open.com**. It reaches the maintainers.

Tell us what happened and what you would like to see happen. We will listen
first, and we will not repeat what you tell us more widely than is needed to
act on it. If your report is about a maintainer, say so — we will keep that
person out of handling it.

Depending on what happened, we may do nothing beyond hearing you out, have a
private word, ask for an apology, or remove someone from the project. We would
rather resolve something with a conversation than a ban, but a project where
people are made unwelcome is worse than a smaller one.

## Scope and spirit

This document is short because a long one invites lawyering, and the cases that
matter are rarely the ones a rulebook anticipated. If you are wondering whether
something is within the spirit of it, it probably is not, and asking first
costs nothing.
